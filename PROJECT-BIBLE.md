# UCLA Century Project Bible

## Purpose

Build a century-long history one calendar year at a time, emphasizing how the institution actually functioned: people, rooms, routines, rules, improvisations, mistakes, and small incidents alongside major events.

## Permanent research standards

- Research only the selected calendar year. Use adjacent years only for clearly marked continuity or retrospective context.
- Prefer newspapers, catalogs, yearbooks, directories, maps, minutes, timetables, photographs, government records, and other contemporary documents.
- Do not invent facts, quotations, dialogue, motives, scenes, dates, or causal links.
- Preserve conflicting names, scores, dates, and accounts; do not harmonize them without evidence.
- Every quotation must be verbatim and include publication, date, page or locator, and URL.
- Maintain an evidence matrix, quote bank, contradiction audit, image manifest, bibliography, and open-questions list.

## Narrative and production standards

- The finished narration should be chronological, observant, human-scale, and quietly quirky rather than mythic or promotional.
- One narrator voice. No music or sound effects in the script.
- Visual descriptions remain separate from narration.
- Archival material is credited. Generated scenes are explicitly labeled as illustrations.
- Do not imitate or name a living or deceased artist; describe visual traits instead.
- NotebookLM is a presentation and analysis layer, not the source of truth.

## Continuity fields

For each year record what continued, changed, failed, disappeared, and remained unresolved from the previous year. Record material that belongs to a later year instead of using it early.
